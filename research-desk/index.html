Mini Golf Canada Research Desk

The organizational byline responsible for Mini Golf Canada's source checks, directory analysis, guides and corrections.

The Mini Golf Canada Research Desk is an organizational byline operated by Nocturnal Devs. It identifies responsibility for source selection, data analysis, directory copy, planning guides, tools and corrections without inventing a named writer or implying that every venue was visited in person.

What the Research Desk does

  • Compares venue-controlled information with municipal, tourism and structured public business sources.
  • Separates explicit course facts from general planning assumptions.
  • Rejects ambiguous attraction prices, copied review text and unsupported superlatives.
  • Maintains the publishing gate for weak, conflicting and non-course profiles.
  • Builds original Canadian planning guides, directory analysis and browser tools.

How a source check works

Automated extraction, including Crawl4AI, helps retrieve and normalize source text. A parser looks for explicit mini-golf context around details such as hole counts, indoor or outdoor setting, seasonality, booking and on-site activities. The resulting page paraphrases narrow facts and links to the source; it does not republish raw pages.

A crawler failure is neutral. It does not prove that a venue closed or that a fact is false. Established accurate ranking pages stay available while a stronger source is sought unless a current official source creates a direct conflict. Weak records are withheld from normal discovery, and records known to represent a different business type are excluded from recommendations and search indexing.

Three separate quality gates

The build does not treat “page exists,” “page can be indexed” and “page can carry advertising” as the same decision. A source-status page can preserve an old bookmark while remaining noindex and outside future ad inventory. An established accurate course URL can remain searchable while its evidence is improved. The publisher-readiness gate is stricter: it requires source-confirmed mini golf, multiple linked references, concrete course detail and a useful mix of planning evidence before advertising code is eligible to load.

This separation protects visitors and search continuity without using weak pages to expand monetized inventory. It also prevents word count from becoming a substitute for substance. Repeated generic questions do not raise a page's evidence level; course-specific facts, local comparisons, coverage limits and transparent sources do.

How claims are limited

  • A category or map pin can establish a lead, but it does not by itself establish a current course format.
  • A missing feature is recorded as unknown, not as evidence that the feature is unavailable.
  • A weekly schedule does not establish holiday hours, weather operation, private events or final tee time.
  • An accessibility label does not establish the surface, slope, width or step condition of every hole.
  • A public rating is dated context from another platform, not a Mini Golf Canada review or recommendation.
  • A price is published only when the source clearly labels a mini-golf amount and relevant player category.

Original analysis

The Canada directory data report analyzes regional coverage, source levels, field completeness, city concentration and course-format signals in the site's own dataset. Listing pages calculate local feature counts, straight-line proximity and evidence coverage from the normalized records. These calculations are original directory analysis, not claims copied from venue pages.

The planning guides and browser tools address decisions that a raw listing cannot answer alone: selecting relevant nearby results, estimating a realistic round window, comparing total party cost, checking accessibility needs and running a consistent tournament. Method limits are published beside the results so an estimate is not mistaken for a venue guarantee.

Review dates and material changes

A listing's visible source-check date follows the underlying record, not the site build time. Rebuilding a page does not make old evidence new. A modification date changes when a substantive listing fact, quality classification, source set or editorial analysis changes. Corrections involving closure, relocation, wrong identity or an official-source conflict take priority over routine enrichment.

Corrections and contact

Source-backed corrections are prioritized. Include the listing URL, requested change, exact venue identity and a venue-controlled or local-authority source. The evidence is checked against duplicate records and current official information before a page date or public claim changes. Contact the Research Desk at nocturnaldevs@gmail.com or use the correction link on the listing. Ownership and technical responsibility remain with Nocturnal Devs.
