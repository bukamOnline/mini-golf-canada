Start with the decision you need to make

The right guide depends on what could make the outing fail. A nearby-search question starts with distance and relevance. A family visit may depend on pace, washrooms and team size. An outdoor course needs a weather and season check, while a glow venue may require questions about darkness, sound and animated effects. Event planning adds capacity, deposits, food rules and cancellation terms.

These guides are written as decision aids rather than lists of venues. They explain which facts can be compared in the directory, which claims need a current operator source, and where a rating or photograph is too limited to answer the question on its own.

How the guides use directory evidence

Mini Golf Canada separates stable descriptive facts from details that change quickly. A venue-controlled page can support a course format, number of holes, theme or attraction mix. Municipal and regional tourism pages can confirm a public park, seasonal attraction or local destination. Structured public profiles help with locations, dated schedules and review-count context, but they do not replace a current official source for admission, booking or accessibility arrangements.

The site does not copy individual reviews or turn third-party ratings into its own star recommendation. Review themes are treated as prompts for what a visitor may want to verify. Prices are published only when a source clearly labels a mini-golf rate; unclear bundles, old promotions and unrelated attraction amounts are left out.

When a course profile and a guide work together

Use a guide to build the checklist, then apply it on the course page. Each maintained profile now shows source-backed facts, public-feedback context, admission questions, a dated weekly schedule when available, nearby comparisons and a clear account of what the page can and cannot verify. City pages place those profiles side by side without calling the highest rating the best course.

For a same-day outing, the venue remains the final authority. For planning and comparison, the directory provides a consistent layer that makes missing information visible instead of filling gaps with guesses. Operators and visitors can submit a sourced correction from every listing.
