Accessible Mini Golf Planning

Questions for planning a mini golf visit around mobility, vision, hearing, sensory and support needs.

By Mini Golf Canada Research DeskUpdated August 7, 2026

Quick answer: an accessible entrance does not prove that every hole is accessible. Ask about the complete course route, surfaces, slopes, passages, lighting, sound, seating, washrooms and support-person policy.

Separate building access from course access

A public profile may identify accessible parking or an entrance while the mini golf route includes steps, narrow bridges, curbs or loose ground. Ask whether a player can complete the holes in order without leaving the accessible route and whether any obstacles require reaching, bending or stepping onto raised surfaces.

Questions that produce useful answers
NeedAsk the venue
MobilityWhat is the narrowest passage, steepest slope and usual surface? Are all holes step-free?
SeatingIs seating available on or beside the route, and can a player pause without blocking groups?
VisionHow bright is the course, are edges high-contrast, and can a companion assist at each hole?
HearingAre instructions available in writing, and are alarms or game cues primarily audible?
SensoryAre there flashing lights, darkness, strong sound, crowds or moving props? Is a quieter time available?
SupportWhat is the admission rule for a support person who is assisting rather than playing?

Describe the requirement precisely

“Is it accessible?” invites a broad answer. A concrete question such as “Can a 76-centimetre wheelchair complete all 18 holes without steps?” is easier for staff to verify. For a new or complex venue, ask for a manager or request current route photos. Record the date and name of the person who confirmed a critical arrangement.

Plan arrival and an exit option

Confirm parking distance, door type, check-in queue, accessible washroom location and where equipment is collected. Ask whether a player can leave and rejoin the route. A sensory or fatigue plan works better when the group knows where a quieter waiting area is before play begins.

How the directory uses accessibility signals

Mini Golf Canada shows an accessibility tag only when a public source contains a related signal. That tag is a prompt to investigate, not a certification of the course. Use it to build a shortlist, then obtain the venue-specific answers your group needs. Corrections with an official accessibility page can be submitted from any listing.
