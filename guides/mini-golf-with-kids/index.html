Mini Golf With Kids: A Practical Guide

How to plan teams, pace, safety and expectations for a mini golf outing with children.

By Mini Golf Canada Research DeskUpdated August 7, 2026

Quick answer: choose a setting the child can tolerate, keep teams small, explain two safety rules, and make finishing every hole optional for the youngest players. The best first round is one they want to repeat.

Match the course to the child

Planning by stage rather than a strict age rule
Player needCourse signal to look forUseful adjustment
Short attention spanNine holes, simple route, visible finishSkip scoring and stop before frustration

Teach safety before scoring

Putters stay below knee height, and players wait until the group ahead has left the hole. Those two rules prevent most avoidable problems. An adult should manage turns near water, stairs, steep slopes or moving obstacles. Ask the venue about stroller storage and whether small children who are not playing need admission.

Keep the group moving

Teams of three or four are easier to supervise than one large family group. Let the next child get ready while the current player finishes, but keep everyone behind the ball. A maximum of six strokes per hole is a common casual rule that limits bottlenecks. For a first outing, count favourite holes or successful shots instead of a total score.

Pack for the actual setting

Outdoor courses may need sun protection, water, insect repellent and footwear for wet surfaces. Indoor glow courses can be dark, loud or visually intense. Food and drinks may be prohibited on the route even when sold on site. Contact the venue about washrooms, seating, change tables and re-entry when those details matter.

End on a good note

Do not force the last holes if the group is finished emotionally before it is finished numerically. A short positive visit has more value than proving everyone completed 18.
