Mini Golf With Kids: A Practical Guide

How to plan teams, pace, safety and expectations for a mini golf outing with children.

By Mini Golf Canada Research DeskUpdated September 7, 2026

The useful family comparison is not simply which venue says all ages. Check the child's age on the visit date, who must pay to accompany them, how much activity the ticket includes and whether the setting suits them. These named examples show how the same family can need different plans.

Operator publications and local-authority sources checked September 7, 2026. Planning analysis by the Mini Golf Canada Research Desk; not firsthand visits or guaranteed current offers.

An accompanying adult can change the bill

At Scarborough Putting Edge, every person entering the course needs paid admission and the branch is cashless. Four players plus an accompanying adult therefore require five $16 admissions, an $80 subtotal before tax. At Wizards' Golf in Niagara Falls, the published non-golfer ticket is $3.99 plus HST. These are different operators and different policies: do not transfer a spectator discount from one to the other. Confirm any support-person arrangement separately.

Sources: Scarborough entry and payment rules; Wizards' Golf non-golfer ticket.

A thirteenth birthday does not affect every course equally

Within Range Burlington charges $10.62 plus HST for ages 4-12 and $11.50 plus HST for ages 13-17. A child's thirteenth birthday adds $0.88 before HST there. Coast to Coast in Woodstock keeps its $12 youth band through fourteen, so the same birthday does not increase its standard youth ticket. At Woodstock, children five and under also require a paid accompanying adult. Use actual ages rather than an undefined family-of-four assumption.

Sources: Within Range age bands; Coast to Coast age and supervision rules.

Separate a mixed activity from a conventional round

Le Grand Mini-golf in Quebec City combines fifteen putting holes and three mini-hockey holes. The operator suggests roughly 90 minutes to two hours and describes suitability from about ages four to five with an adult. Tell children about the change of activity before booking, and do not reserve a tight meal or parking window based on a generic short-round estimate. Younger children's supervised free-play opportunities are not the same as a promise they can complete every station.

Sources: Le Grand course and family FAQ.

Ask about the specific surprise, not just the theme

Haunted Manor in Victoria welcomes all ages but describes ghostly imagery and two mild air-powered effects. Staff can identify their locations before a round. That information supports a concrete conversation with a child who dislikes surprises; it does not establish a quiet or sensory-friendly session. Its maximum of five guests per tee-time slot also means a family group of six needs additional back-to-back slots.

Sources: Haunted Manor effects and booking FAQ.

Make a winter indoor outing comfortable

Fluo S'amuse asks visitors to remove outdoor footwear in winter or snowy conditions. Bring indoor shoes to change into, or use its stated socks option; lockers are $2. A dry indoor activity can still need preparation at the door. Choose the single-round ticket first unless the family genuinely wants repeated play and the venue confirms its attendance-dependent unlimited option is available.

Sources: Fluo footwear and ticket conditions.
